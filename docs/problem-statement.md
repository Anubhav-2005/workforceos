# Problem statement

## The gap between AI tools and real work

Teams are adopting AI quickly, but most tools still behave like isolated utilities. One tool reviews a resume, another drafts an email, and another answers a support request. A person remains responsible for copying context, deciding what happens next, and checking whether the work was completed.

That coordination cost grows as the number of tools grows.

## Who feels the problem

Small and mid-sized teams feel it most. They want the leverage of specialized AI without hiring an operations team to supervise disconnected automations. Functional leaders also need confidence that high-impact decisions—hiring, pricing, customer commitments, and escalations—remain visible and reviewable.

## What is broken today

1. **Work is fragmented.** AI output lives in separate products and conversations.
2. **Handoffs are manual.** Context is copied between recruiting, sales, support, and operations.
3. **Ownership is unclear.** Teams cannot easily see which AI is working, waiting, or blocked.
4. **Human review is bolted on.** Approvals often happen outside the automation and leave no clear trail.
5. **Impact is hard to measure.** Leaders see tool usage, but not completed work or reclaimed capacity.

## The opportunity

The useful unit is not another chatbot. It is a coordinated workforce in which specialized AI employees can receive tasks, pass structured work to one another, pause for human judgment, and report outcomes in one place.

WorkforceOS demonstrates that operating model.

## MVP success criteria

The hackathon MVP succeeds when a judge can:

- navigate a coherent AI workforce dashboard;
- upload a real PDF resume and receive structured OpenAI analysis;
- review and decide on a candidate;
- run a cross-employee workflow with a human approval checkpoint;
- see visible execution state and an auditable log;
- use the product on a phone or desktop without broken layouts.
